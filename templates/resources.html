Key Risk Factors

Family History

Having a close relative with pancreatic cancer increases your risk significantly.

Smoking

Smoking is one of the most significant risk factors for pancreatic cancer.

Obesity

Being overweight or obese increases the risk of developing pancreatic cancer.

Diabetes

Long-standing diabetes, especially new-onset in older adults, is a risk factor.

Prevention Tips

Quit Smoking

If you smoke, quitting is one of the best things you can do for your health.

Maintain Healthy Weight

Aim for a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 through diet and exercise.

Limit Alcohol

Moderate alcohol consumption to reduce pancreatic stress.

Regular Checkups

Schedule regular health screenings with your doctor.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the early warning signs of pancreatic cancer?
Early warning signs may include jaundice (yellowing of skin/eyes), abdominal or back pain, unexplained weight loss, loss of appetite, and new-onset diabetes. However, these symptoms can also be caused by other conditions.

How can I reduce my risk of pancreatic cancer?
You can reduce your risk by not smoking, maintaining a healthy weight, exercising regularly, eating a balanced diet rich in fruits and vegetables, limiting alcohol consumption, and managing diabetes if you have it.

Who should get screened for pancreatic cancer?
Screening is typically recommended for people with a strong family history of pancreatic cancer, those with certain genetic syndromes, or people with hereditary conditions that increase risk. Consult your doctor about your individual risk.
